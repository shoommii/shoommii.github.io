١Prologue · The namesake
الكنديal-Kindi
philosopher of ninth-century Baghdad, after whom Kindi is named

In ninth-century Baghdad, the most advanced science in the world was arriving from somewhere else, in Greek manuscripts and Indian mathematics. Al-Kindi refused to leave it foreign. He built the vocabulary, argued with the ideas and turned them toward new questions, until the knowledge belonged to the language that had received it. The Arabs came to call him their philosopher.

Codebreakers remember him for something else. He wrote the earliest known account of frequency analysis, cracking ciphers by counting how often each letter appears. He saw that language has structure, and that anyone who understands the structure can read what others cannot.

The parallel is deliberate. The defining science of this century is being written in other languages and run in data centers thousands of miles from Cairo. Egypt can import it as it comes, or it can do what al-Kindi did. We named the company after him because we intend to do the second.

Frequency analysis

Type anything, in English or Arabic. Its structure shows up the same way it did for al-Kindi: a few letters carry most of the language.

٢Our mission
Kindi exists to give Egypt, and then the Arab world, artificial intelligence it owns: models that speak its language, run on its soil, and answer to its own institutions.
LanguageSoilInstitutions

We believe intelligence is becoming infrastructure, as fundamental to a country as power, water and telecommunications. A nation that rents all of its intelligence from abroad rents a part of its sovereignty along with it. Egypt has the talent, the institutions and the need to own this layer. What it has lacked is a company whose only job is to build it.

٣
Why now

The institutions that most need AI cannot buy it as it is sold today.

Egypt’s most important institutions want to use AI and mostly cannot. A bank cannot send customer conversations to a foreign cloud. A ministry cannot put citizen records in a data center it does not control. A utility serving tens of millions of people cannot rely on a model that stumbles over Egyptian Arabic.

The global platforms were not built for this. Their models treat Arabic as a translation target, their compute sits outside the country, and their accountability ends at a terms-of-service page. The Gulf states have answered the question for themselves with vast public investment. Egypt, the largest Arabic-speaking nation, still has no answer of its own.

That gap is our reason for existing.

٤
What we build

The sovereign AI layer.

Kindi builds the sovereign AI layer: the models, the harness that governs them, and the on-soil deployment that keeps every byte inside the country. We sell the foundation, not the applications built on it.

Our models start from the best open weights in the world and are shaped for Egyptian Arabic, government language and the data of the sectors we serve. Our harness is where the accountability lives, with guardrails, audit trails, residency controls and the configuration a regulator can inspect. Our deployments run on Egyptian compute operated by Egyptian partners, so the question of where the data goes has a single, simple answer.

We deliberately leave the applications to others. Banks, integrators and public institutions know their own problems better than we do, and they should build on a foundation they trust. Every application we demonstrate exists to prove the platform, then passes to the partner or customer who will own it.

٥
Who we serve first

Institutions that cannot say no to sovereignty.

We serve the institutions that cannot say no to sovereignty. They share four traits: their data cannot leave Egypt, they operate in Egyptian Arabic at high volume, they have a mandate and a budget owner who can act, and they already sit close to the compute we run on.

Banks come first, because regulation has already settled the residency question and their customers speak to them in Arabic millions of times a day. Telcos come alongside them, as both our largest customers and the hosts of our infrastructure. State authorities and utilities follow, the institutions that touch citizens directly and cannot buy from a foreign cloud even if they wanted to.

Inside each of them, our champion is the leader responsible for digital transformation, standing next to the leader responsible for risk. Kindi is the first AI story both of them can sign.

Egypt is our home and our proving ground. The Gulf is our second market and, before that, the source of the capital that lets this infrastructure scale across the region.

How we grow

Partners, proof and the platform itself.

We grow through partners, proof and the platform itself.

أPartners

Partners carry us into rooms we could not enter alone. Advisory firms bring the relationships with banks and government, telcos and data center operators bring the compute and the national footprint, and integrators build the applications. Each of them earns more because Kindi exists, which is the only partnership model that lasts.

بProof

Proof comes before persuasion. We publish how our models perform on Egyptian Arabic so anyone can check the claim, we write openly about what sovereign AI requires, and every deployment is built to become the reference for the next one.

جPlatform

The platform compounds. Each new use case runs on the same foundation, each customer strengthens the models and the benchmark, and each partner widens the ground the next one stands on.

A country that owns its intelligence.
