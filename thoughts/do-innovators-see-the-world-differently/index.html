Do Innovators See the World Differently?

Hashem AlyMay 21, 20203 min read

Innovations such as the iPhone, Facebook, SpaceX reusable rockets and Google search probably took a tremendous amount of work to create. But what is also important is that they had key revolutionary features that required the people who created them to fight the norms and defy the status quo. With the iPhone it was getting rid of the stylus and introducing multi-touch technology. With Facebook it was handling tremendous amounts of data and users without crashing, with Google it was their search algorithm Pagerank and with Space X it is re-usable rockets.

The Beginners Approach

The products had key revolutionary features but the people behind them, the innovators that have the vision are the driving force behind these products. If you listen to Tony Fadell a former Apple employee talking about creating the first iPod it instantly becomes evident that innovators approach problems differently. Steve Jobs instilled the an idea within his top management to have a ‘beginner’s mentality’. As Fadell puts it “as human beings we have limited brain power which means our brain is trained to encode the everyday things we do into habits, so we can free up space to learn new things”. This process is called habituation. Habituation is necessary to get you through your day but during times of innovation it’s important to keep that process aside and look at things with a fresh approach. This is what Jobs meant when he demanded employees to stay beginners or have a beginners mentality.

The beginners mentality was used with the iPod. Before 2001 every electronic device was shipped with an empty battery and customers had to charge it before using it. When Apple approached the problem with a beginners mentality saw how customers were frustrated with the fact that they have to charge it for an hour or two before using the product. As a result Apple decided to charge the iPods in the factory before shipping them so that customers can use it straight out of the box. This made a whole lot of difference and customer loved that they could use Apple products straight out of the box. After the success of the iPod was evident almost every electronic device post 2001 is shipped with a charged battery.

Reasoning By First Principle

Another visionary that solves problems differently is Elon Musk. He has a radical idea that humans should be a multi planetary species and that we will live on Mars in our lifetime. Sounds crazy right? But Musk has thought this through and concluded that in order to do so the cost of producing and using rockets should be lowered significantly so that he can move people and cargo to and from Mars at a reasonable cost. The way he approached this problem is key. His theory is to look at a problem and then ‘reason by first principle’ rather than by analogy.

To reason by analogy is to look at an existing solution and add a small iteration. To reason by first principle on the other hand is to is boil something down to the fundamental truths, or what appear to be the fundamental truths, and reasoning up from there. To build a cost efficient rocket Musk used the first principle approach. He dismantled every piece of the puzzle and looked at how the rockets materials can be cheaper, how the engine can be designed differently etc.... This resulted in creating a reusable rocket with cheaper materials and in turn lowered the cost of building a rocket from $500 million and is used just once to a rocket that costs $60 million and can be used multiple times.

So do innovators see the world differently? Yes they do, or at least they approach problems and solve them in a different way.
