Trip 1 · today

When I started Masla7tak I thought the fix was better information. If people knew exactly what to bring, they wouldn’t waste the trip. That turned out to be only half right. The official requirements aren’t secret, and most of them are already on مصر الرقمية. What’s missing is what the window actually asks for, which can differ from the official list, from one office to another, and from what it was a few months ago. The only people who know it are the ones who just went.

٣
What it does today

اعرف ورقك قبل ما تروح

Masla7tak today does one thing. You tell it what you need, whether that’s renewing your ID or passport, a driving licence or a فيش, and it gives you a list for your case: the papers, the fees and the steps in order, written the way you’d explain it to a friend. The list changes with your situation, so a renewal with the same details isn’t treated like one where something has changed.

The part I care about most is the small notes under each list. Take a copy of your ID even though it isn’t on the official list, because some offices ask for it. Bring your old passport even if it’s expired. If your marital status isn’t on your ID, bring proof of it from the start so the request doesn’t stall. Don’t get your فيش too early, because it’s only valid for three months. None of that is the official requirement, and any of it can cost you a second trip.

Your listfor your case

The papers, fees and steps are the official part. The notes are the part that saves the trip.

Each list also says when it was last reviewed, and it tells you plainly what can be done online. For a passport, the form, payment and appointment can be done from home, but fingerprints, photos and pickup still need you at the office. For a فيش, if your fingerprints are already registered, you can order it online and have it delivered without going anywhere, and we’ve checked that this actually works. Sometimes the most useful answer is that you don’t need to go at all.

What I got wrong

Trying to do the errand for you.

The lists were the part that worked. Where I went wrong was trying to go further too quickly.

First I tried adding people: runners and experts who would carry the errand for you. It worked for a handful of cases and turned into an operations mess as soon as there were more. Then I tried live sessions on WhatsApp, where someone would guide you through it. Same problem. Both needed someone like me involved in every errand, and that doesn’t grow. So the lists stayed at the centre, and anything new has to sit on top of them without needing a person in the middle.

What I’m testing

An agent.

The open question is whether Masla7tak can do some of these errands with you, not just tell you how. I’m testing that with an agent on one service, the فيش وتشبيه application on مصر الرقمية. I picked it because it can already be done fully online, so the hard part isn’t the office, it’s getting through the portal. The agent goes through it and fills in the forms while you watch, explaining what it’s doing as it goes.

I don’t know if this is the answer. I’m not sure people will want it, pay for it, or use it more than once. It’s an experiment and I’m treating it like one.

Will people want itPay for itUse it more than once

What I have decided is how it should behave. You log in yourself and enter your own OTP, so it never has your credentials. Before you pay, it shows you everything it filled in, and you can fix anything or cancel. It stops before payment and again before submitting, and those stops are hard-coded rather than left to the model. People are careful with anything involving their national ID, their money and their record, and they should be. I don’t think anyone should hand those over to a product just because it says it’s safe.

    What compounds

    Learning from people who’ve just been.

    The notes under each list are the start of something bigger. What I want is for more of them to come straight from people after their errand. What were they asked for? Were they sent back? Had something changed since last month? If enough people answer, the next person gets a more accurate list than any official page can give them, and every wasted trip makes someone else’s shorter.

    That’s still an idea. I haven’t measured it properly, and until I do I can’t say it works. I’ve been wrong about this problem a few times already, so I’d rather say that now than later.

    Even online, there’s still a gap.

    Closing

    Why I keep coming back to it.

    A lot of work is going into putting government services online, and it’s helping. But even online, there’s still a gap between what a service says and what actually happens to you when you use it.

    Masla7tak is my attempt to close it from the citizen’s side, one مصلحة at a time, starting with the simplest promise I could make:

    اعرف ورقك قبل ما تروح
    Know your papers before you go.
